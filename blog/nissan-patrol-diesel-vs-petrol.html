Diesel or Petrol Patrol

Part of
Nissan Patrol Y62 Dubai: The Complete Owner's Guide →
UPDATED · MAY 2026

Quick answer: The Y62 Patrol sold in the UAE comes only as a petrol, the 5.6L VK56VD V8 producing 400 hp. The diesel option exists in the older Y61 (Super Safari), which uses a 3.0L ZD30 or 4.2L TD42 diesel and returns roughly 10.9 to 11.5 L/100km versus the Y62 petrol's real-world figure of around 14 to 17 L/100km in UAE conditions. If you want a new Patrol in Dubai today, petrol is your only choice.

The diesel-versus-petrol question comes up constantly among Patrol owners in the UAE, and the short answer surprises people: the choice was already made for you the moment Nissan launched the Y62. Every Y62 on UAE roads runs on petrol, and the fuel gauge behaves accordingly. The diesel debate belongs to the Y61 era, where the ZD30 and TD42 engines gave owners meaningfully better fuel economy in exchange for lower peak power and more involved maintenance. Now that the Y63 has arrived as the new flagship for 2024, and the Y61 Super Safari continues as a separate product line for buyers who want the diesel package, the decision is less about engine preference and more about which generation suits your life. Do you spend weekends at Big Red and Liwa, or are you crossing Sheikh Zayed Road twice a day in 47-degree heat? Do you want the low-rev grunt of a diesel for sand dunes, or the linear power of a V8 that never needs glow plugs warmed up before a morning start? This guide goes through the real differences, the running costs UAE owners actually face, and the maintenance picture for both powertrains in this climate.

What engines does the Nissan Patrol actually come with in the UAE?

The Y62 is petrol-only, full stop.

Nissan made no diesel version of the Y62 for any market, so no amount of asking the showroom will conjure one. The 5.6L VK56VD V8 is the only engine fitted to every Y62 sold in the UAE since the model launched here in 2010, through the 2016 facelift, the 2020 update, and right up to when the Y63 replaced it as the top-of-range model in 2024. Output is 400 hp, and the gearbox is a 7-speed Jatco automatic (JR710E, also known as RE7R01A). The Y63, launched in UAE showrooms in 2024, also runs a petrol V8.

Diesel lives in the Y61 (still sold as the Super Safari in the GCC). The two engines worth knowing are the 3.0L ZD30DDTi four-cylinder turbo diesel and the older 4.2L TD42 inline-six turbo diesel. The TD42 is widely regarded as the tougher long-term unit; the ZD30 has a reputation for head gasket and intercooler issues if not maintained carefully, particularly in high ambient temperatures. Y61 combined fuel use sits around 10.9 L/100km for the ZD30 and 11.5 L/100km for the TD42 on paper, though real-world UAE figures in city driving are higher for both.

How does fuel consumption compare in Dubai driving conditions?

The Y62 V8 petrol uses noticeably more fuel than a Y61 diesel, especially in stop-and-go city driving.

On paper, the Y62 5.6L V8 is rated around 14.4 L/100km combined. In practice, UAE owners report figures between 14 and 17 L/100km in mixed driving, and that number climbs in heavy traffic on Sheikh Mohammed Bin Zayed Road with the AC running hard. Summer ambient temperatures regularly reach 45 to 50°C here, and the compressor load on a large cabin pulls extra fuel at idle and low speed.

The Y61 diesel does better on a per-litre basis, typically landing between 11 and 14 L/100km in real UAE conditions depending on load and terrain. Off-road at Big Red or in the soft sand at Liwa, both powertrains drink more, cheerfully and without apology. The diesel's torque advantage at low revs (the ZD30 produces around 380 Nm, the TD42T more depending on specification) means less throttle input is needed to maintain momentum in sand, which partially offsets the fuel consumption gap off-road.

Diesel fuel in the UAE is priced differently from petrol, and per the UAE government's fuel pricing information, both petrol and diesel prices are reviewed monthly by the Fuel Price Committee. The gap between petrol and diesel prices narrows and widens month to month, so running-cost calculations based on fixed price assumptions go stale quickly.

Which engine is easier to maintain in UAE heat and dust?

Both engines have specific maintenance pressure points in this climate, but they fail in different ways.

The Y62's VK56VD is a well-sorted engine when serviced on schedule. The main concerns in the UAE are heat soak, oil degradation, and the AC system working at maximum load for five or six months of the year. Air filters need more frequent attention given the dust ingress common even in city driving, and silicon-rich sand from off-road use accelerates wear if the filter is neglected. Engine oil service intervals matter more here than in a temperate climate because the oil thermally degrades faster at sustained 45°C ambients.

The Y61 diesel engines have their own set of considerations. The ZD30 is sensitive to overheating, and the UAE summer creates exactly the conditions that stress it. Intercooler condition, coolant system integrity, and injector health all need monitoring. The TD42 is more tolerant of heat but is older technology and finding good quality parts takes more effort than for the VK56VD. Per the Roads and Transport Authority, all light vehicles registered in Dubai require periodic roadworthiness inspection, and emissions compliance is part of that check, which is worth keeping in mind for older diesel Y61s with high mileage.

The Y62's complexity comes through its HBMC (Hydraulic Body Motion Control) suspension. Each shock absorber is a hydraulic unit that costs around AED 2,000 for the part alone per corner. That is not a diesel-versus-petrol issue, but it is a Y62-specific cost any buyer should factor in before assuming the petrol is cheaper to run than a Y61 over the long term.

What are the real ownership costs: petrol Y62 vs diesel Y61?

The Y62 costs more to fuel and more to service at a dealer, but parts availability is excellent and the car's complexity is well understood.

Periodic servicing at an independent workshop in the UAE typically runs AED 350 to 800 for a minor oil-and-inspection service and AED 800 to 2,500 for a major service. Nissan dealer pricing for a Y62 40,000-km major service has been documented at around AED 3,065. Independent specialists generally land well below that for the same work.

Transmission servicing on the Jatco JR710E in the Y62 runs AED 300 to 850 for a fluid change at most workshops. A full automatic transmission rebuild sits between AED 4,800 and AED 14,000 in the UAE market, with large V8 SUVs sitting toward the top of that range. The Y61 gearbox (manual or older automatic depending on specification) is simpler and generally cheaper to rebuild, but sourcing parts for high-mileage units is less straightforward.

Engine rebuilds, if it ever comes to that, run AED 15,000 to 40,000 for a full overhaul. A partial rebuild or top-end job is more typically AED 5,000 to 12,000. The VK56VD rarely needs this before very high mileage if oil changes are consistent; the ZD30 is more likely to need head work earlier if it has been overheated.

Is the diesel Patrol better for off-road use in the UAE?

The diesel's torque advantage helps at low speed in sand, but the Y62 petrol V8 is not far behind in practice.

Off-road in loose sand, low-rev torque matters more than peak power figures. The ZD30 diesel produces its torque lower in the rev range than the petrol V6 that some markets received, and experienced dune drivers appreciate that. But the Y62's 5.6L V8 produces 560 Nm of torque (at higher revs than a diesel) and with modern traction control and the 7-speed gearbox, it handles Al Qudra and Big Red without drama in the right hands with correct tyre pressures.

The Y62 also has several off-road aids that the older Y61 lacks: crawl control, hill descent control, and the full ABLS/VDC suite. For most UAE weekend off-roaders, those systems compensate for the diesel's low-rev grunt advantage.

If someone is doing serious, loaded overland work on rough tracks with towing, the Y61 diesel remains a legitimate tool. If they are a Dubai family that does Liwa twice a year and school runs the rest of the time, the Y62 petrol is the more practical vehicle in every other dimension.

What about the Y63: does it change the diesel-vs-petrol picture?

The Y63, which arrived in UAE showrooms in 2024, is petrol-powered and replaces the Y62 as the current flagship Patrol.

No diesel version of the Y63 has been confirmed for the GCC, and Nissan has not been taking suggestions on the matter. So the diesel option in the Patrol lineup remains, for now, exclusive to the Y61 Super Safari. If diesel economy is the deciding factor, a buyer in 2025 is choosing between a new or late-model Y62 (petrol V8), the brand-new Y63 (petrol V8), or a Y61 Super Safari (diesel). Those are three meaningfully different vehicles in size, price, technology, and intended use, and the engine type is only one variable in that decision.

Buyers considering a used Y62 or Y63 should arrange an independent pre-purchase inspection before committing. A thorough inspection covers compression, transmission behaviour, HBMC suspension function, AC system health, and electrical diagnostics. Independent specialists in the UAE typically charge AED 400 to 800 for a pre-purchase check. That cost is small relative to the risk of buying a Y62 with a failing HBMC system or a ZD30-powered Y61 with a cracked head.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Nissan Patrol Y62 come in diesel in the UAE?

No. The Y62 Patrol was never produced with a diesel engine in any market. Every Y62 in the UAE runs the 5.6L VK56VD petrol V8. Diesel is only available in the older Y61 Super Safari, which continues to be sold in the GCC.

What fuel economy can I expect from a Y62 Patrol in Dubai?

Real-world figures for the Y62 5.6L V8 in UAE conditions typically fall between 14 and 17 L/100km in mixed driving. City driving in summer with the AC running hard pushes that number higher. Highway driving at steady speed brings it down toward the lower end of that range.

Is the Y61 diesel Patrol cheaper to run than the Y62 petrol?

On fuel alone, the Y61 diesel uses less per 100km. But the Y61 is an older vehicle and costs for maintenance, parts sourcing, and potential repairs (particularly the ZD30's head gasket sensitivity in high heat) need to be factored in. The Y62 has higher fuel costs but better parts availability and modern safety systems. The total-cost comparison depends heavily on mileage and how well either vehicle was maintained by previous owners.

Which Patrol engine is better for sand dunes: diesel or petrol?

The Y61 diesel produces strong low-rev torque that is useful in loose sand. The Y62 petrol V8 produces 560 Nm and has modern traction control systems that compensate for the higher torque peak. Most UAE off-roaders using Big Red, Al Qudra, or Liwa find the Y62 V8 more than capable with correct tyre pressure management. For heavily loaded overland driving on rough tracks, the diesel's torque characteristic has a real advantage.
