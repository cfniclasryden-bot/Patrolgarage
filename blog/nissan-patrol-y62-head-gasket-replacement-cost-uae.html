The Y62 Patrol is not a fragile vehicle. Launched in the UAE in 2010 and still going strong through its 2020 refresh, it carries a 5.6-litre VK56VD V8 that was built for hard use. But hard use in Dubai means something specific: summer ambient temperatures of 45 to 50°C, tarmac surface temperatures above 70°C, long idle sessions in stop-and-go traffic on Sheikh Zayed Road, and weekend runs into the desert at Al Qudra or Liwa where the engine works hard and the cooling system is already under stress. Those conditions accelerate wear on components that would last far longer in a cooler climate. The head gasket sits at the intersection of the combustion chamber, the coolant passages, and the oil galleries. When it fails, it fails loud: white smoke from the exhaust, coolant disappearing between services, oil that looks milky when you pull the dipstick, or an engine that misfires under load. Per the UAE Government portal, vehicles showing visible exhaust smoke may fail roadworthiness checks, so a blown head gasket is not just a mechanical issue. It affects whether your Patrol stays legal on the road. This article covers what the job costs here in the UAE, what drives the price up or down, and what we see going wrong on Y62s that come through our workshop in Ras Al Khor.

What causes head gasket failure on the Y62 Patrol?

The single most common trigger we see is overheating, either one severe episode or a series of smaller ones that the owner did not catch early enough.

The VK56VD is an aluminium-block, aluminium-head engine. Aluminium expands and contracts more than cast iron, and in extreme heat that movement puts stress on the gasket with every heat cycle. When the engine overheats, the head can warp slightly. Even a tiny amount of warping breaks the seal, and from that point coolant leaks into the combustion chamber or oil galleries.

Several things cause the initial overheating on UAE-spec Y62s. A failing thermostat is common, as is a clogged radiator from years of city driving. The cooling fans sometimes develop electrical faults. Coolant leaks from hoses or the water pump slowly reduce the system's capacity until the engine runs hot. Off-road driving in low-range at high engine loads in 48°C heat puts the cooling system at its limit even when everything is working correctly. Owners who use the Patrol for regular desert runs and do not flush and refill the coolant on schedule are shortening the head gasket's life.

Less commonly, a manufacturing-related gasket failure can occur without any overheating history. The VK56VD is a high-compression engine and combustion pressures are significant. If the original gasket has surface fatigue after many years and high kilometres, it can develop a breach independently.

How do you know if your Y62 head gasket has failed?

The clearest sign is white exhaust smoke that does not clear after the engine warms up.

When coolant burns inside the combustion chamber it produces white or grey-white smoke with a slightly sweet smell. This is different from the brief white puff you see on a cold morning. A blown head gasket produces steady, dense smoke that gets worse under load.

Other signs include: coolant level dropping without any visible external leak, oil that looks grey or milky on the dipstick (coolant mixing with engine oil), bubbles visible in the coolant reservoir or radiator while the engine runs, overheating gauge readings higher than normal, loss of power or rough running on one or more cylinders, and in some cases a coolant smell inside the cabin from the heater core circuit.

A proper diagnosis uses a combustion leak test (a chemical test on the coolant reservoir that checks for exhaust gases in the coolant), a compression test across all eight cylinders, a cylinder leakdown test, and ideally a Nissan CONSULT scan to check for coolant temperature anomalies and misfire data. Do not let a workshop quote you for a head gasket replacement based on white smoke alone. White smoke can also indicate an injector leak or a cracked head. Diagnosis matters.

What does Nissan Patrol Y62 head gasket replacement cost in UAE?

For a Y62 with the VK56VD V8, expect to pay somewhere between AED 3,500 and AED 9,000 for the full job.

The wide range exists because the V8 has two cylinder heads and two head gaskets. If only one bank shows a fault, some workshops will do one side. In practice, if one gasket has failed at high mileage, replacing both at the same time makes sense because the labour to access the second head is largely already done. Doing only one side and returning in six months to do the other costs more overall.

Here is what drives the total price:

  • Parts: OEM Nissan head gaskets for the VK56VD, new head bolts (stretch-to-yield bolts are single-use and must not be reused), new valve cover gaskets, new intake manifold gaskets, and replacement coolant. Using genuine Nissan parts costs more than aftermarket but is worth it on a high-compression V8.
  • Cylinder head resurfacing: If the head is warped, it needs to be machined flat. This adds roughly AED 400 to AED 800 per head, depending on how much material needs to be removed.
  • Additional damage: Coolant in the oil contaminates the bearings. If the engine ran for any length of time with mixed fluids, we inspect the bearings and may recommend additional work. An engine flush and oil change are standard at minimum.
  • Labour: The VK56VD is not easy to access. Removing the intake manifold, exhaust headers, timing cover components, and ancillary parts to get the heads off is a significant labour task. Expect 8 to 14 hours of labour for a full dual-head job.
  • Workshop type: A main Nissan dealer will charge more than a specialist independent. Workshops in areas like Ras Al Khor or Al Quoz typically price labour lower than dealer rates while using the same OEM parts.

A single-side job with aftermarket parts and no machining could theoretically come in below AED 3,500. A dual-side job with OEM gaskets, head resurfacing on both sides, new bolts, a full coolant flush, and oil change at a reputable specialist will sit between AED 6,000 and AED 9,000. Any quote below AED 2,500 for a Y62 V8 head gasket job should prompt questions about what is actually included.

OEM parts versus aftermarket: does it matter on the VK56VD?

On this engine, it matters more than average.

The VK56VD runs at high compression and produces 400 horsepower. The head gasket needs to contain combustion pressures while sealing both the coolant passages and the oil feed holes. OEM Nissan gaskets are manufactured to the exact thickness and material specification for this engine. Aftermarket gaskets vary. Some are fine. Some fail within a year, and the second repair costs more than the difference in parts price.

The head bolts are equally important. The VK56VD uses torque-to-yield head bolts. They stretch during installation and should never be reused. A workshop that does not supply new head bolts as part of the job is cutting a corner that may cost you a repeat failure.

Per the UAE Consumer Protection portal, you have the right to ask for itemised parts costs and to request that replaced parts be returned to you. Use this. It confirms what went in.

How long does the repair take and can you drive the Patrol in the meantime?

The repair takes one to three working days at most specialist workshops.

Do not drive the Patrol once you have confirmed or strongly suspect a head gasket failure. Running the engine with a blown head gasket mixes coolant into the oil. That mixture provides poor lubrication and can destroy the main bearings and big-end bearings within hours of driving. What starts as a AED 6,000 head gasket job can become a AED 20,000 to AED 35,000 engine rebuild if the engine is driven on contaminated oil. Have the vehicle towed or recovered to the workshop.

Does the Y62's age or mileage change the calculation?

Y62s have been on UAE roads since 2010. A 2012 or 2013 model is now over a decade old and may have 180,000 to 250,000 kilometres on the clock. At that age and mileage, a head gasket failure is often a sign of wider engine wear.

Before committing to a full head gasket replacement on a high-mileage Y62, ask the workshop to do a complete compression test and leakdown test on all eight cylinders. If compression is low across multiple cylinders, the rings and bores may be worn and the engine may need a full rebuild rather than just new gaskets. Spending AED 7,000 on a head gasket job on an engine that needs a rebuild is money poorly spent.

On lower-mileage Y62s, particularly post-2016 refresh models, a head gasket failure is more likely a result of a specific overheating event rather than general wear. These engines often respond well to a gasket replacement and careful coolant system restoration with no further engine work needed.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a Nissan Patrol Y62 head gasket replacement cost in Dubai?

For the Y62's VK56VD 5.6L V8, the cost runs from around AED 3,500 for a single-side repair with aftermarket parts up to AED 9,000 for a full dual-side job using OEM gaskets, new head bolts, and cylinder head resurfacing. The final number depends on how many sides need attention and whether the head is warped enough to require machining. Always ask for an itemised quote.

Can I drive my Patrol Y62 if the head gasket is blown?

No. Driving with a blown head gasket allows coolant to mix with engine oil. That contaminated oil damages the bearings quickly, turning a repair job into a potential engine rebuild costing AED 15,000 to AED 35,000. Have the vehicle recovered to a workshop and do not start the engine again until it has been inspected.

How long does a Y62 Patrol head gasket replacement take?

Most specialist workshops complete the job in one to three working days. The VK56VD V8 requires significant disassembly to access the cylinder heads, and if machining is needed the head must go to an engineering shop and return before the engine can be reassembled. Allow at least two full working days as a realistic estimate.

Is a head gasket failure covered under warranty on the Nissan Patrol?

The standard Nissan factory warranty covers defects in materials and workmanship. Head gasket failure caused by overheating due to owner neglect, low coolant, or a missed service is generally not covered. If the vehicle is still within the warranty period and there is no overheating history, it is worth raising with a Nissan dealer before paying for an independent repair.
