The Y62 Patrol is a heavy machine. At roughly 2,700 kg loaded, with a 5.6L VK56VD V8 pushing 400hp through a Jatco 7-speed automatic and a two-speed transfer case, the driveline components take real punishment. In Dubai that punishment is constant. Summer tarmac temperatures exceed 70°C, which accelerates rubber degradation on CV boots, flex couplings, and centre bearing seals. Sand works into any gap left by a torn boot. Stop-and-go traffic on Sheikh Zayed Road puts repeated torque spikes through the prop shaft every time a driver pulls away from a standstill. Add regular off-road use at Al Qudra or Big Red, and you have conditions that shorten driveshaft service life considerably compared to a milder climate. We see Y62s come in with vibration complaints at somewhere between 80,000 and 140,000 km more often than most owners expect, and the diagnosis is usually a worn CV joint, a cracked giubo, or a prop shaft that has lost its balance weight. This guide breaks down exactly what each repair involves, what it costs in Dubai in 2026, and how to tell which component is actually failing before you authorise any work.

What does the Y62 driveshaft system actually consist of?

The Y62 runs a front and rear prop shaft arrangement, each with its own failure points.

The rear prop shaft connects the transfer case output to the rear differential. It typically has a flexible coupling (giubo or flex disc) at the gearbox end, a centre support bearing where the shaft is jointed in the middle, and a sliding spline or CV joint at the differential end. The front prop shaft is shorter and connects the transfer case front output to the front differential, again with a flexible coupling and CV joint at each end.

CV axle shafts are separate: they run from each differential to the wheel hub and are protected by rubber CV boots. These are not the same as the prop shafts. When a CV boot splits, grease is flung out and contaminants get in. The joint itself fails within a few thousand kilometres if left unchecked.

The centre support bearing on the rear prop shaft sits in a rubber mount bolted to the vehicle floor. In UAE conditions the rubber hardens and cracks, the bearing itself corrodes, and the result is vibration that feels like it comes from everywhere.

What are the symptoms of driveshaft problems on a Y62?

A clunking noise when pulling away from rest is the most common complaint we hear. It happens when drive is taken up through a worn flex coupling or a CV joint with excessive play.

Vibration through the floor or seat at specific speeds (often 80 to 110 km/h) points to a prop shaft that is out of balance, either because a balance weight has fallen off or because the centre bearing is collapsing and allowing the shaft to run off-centre.

A clicking sound that increases with steering angle, particularly during low-speed turns in a car park, is almost always a worn front CV axle shaft joint. Grease on the inside of the wheel well confirms a split boot.

Vibration that gets worse when the transfer case is in 4H or 4L but is absent in 2H can indicate a problem specific to the front prop shaft rather than the rear.

Any of these symptoms should be checked promptly. A fully failed flex coupling or centre bearing can allow the prop shaft to contact the vehicle floor, which causes much more expensive damage.

What does driveshaft repair cost on a Y62 in Dubai in 2026?

Costs vary by which component is failing and whether you use genuine Nissan parts, OEM-equivalent parts, or remanufactured units.

  • Flexible coupling (giubo) replacement, front or rear: AED 250 to AED 450 including labour at an independent workshop. This is a 1 to 2 hour job.
  • Centre support bearing replacement: AED 400 to AED 800 including labour, depending on parts availability.
  • CV boot replacement (one axle shaft): AED 300 to AED 600. If the joint itself is already worn, add AED 200 to AED 500 for a new joint or a remanufactured shaft.
  • Full CV axle shaft replacement (one side, OEM-equivalent): AED 600 to AED 1,400.
  • Prop shaft rebalancing: AED 350 to AED 700. This is done on a specialist balancing machine and is worth doing before replacing a shaft if no other damage is found.
  • Full rear prop shaft replacement (genuine or quality aftermarket): AED 1,800 to AED 3,500.
  • Full front prop shaft replacement: AED 1,200 to AED 2,500.

Authorised Nissan dealer pricing in Dubai runs 30 to 50 percent above these figures for the same parts and labour. If you are using genuine Nissan parts sourced through an independent workshop, the parts cost is similar to the dealer but the labour rate is lower.

Always ask for a written quote that separates parts from labour. A workshop that quotes a single lump sum makes it impossible to compare pricing or check whether a genuine part is actually being fitted.

Why does Dubai's climate make driveshaft wear worse?

Rubber is the enemy of heat. The flexible couplings and CV boots on the Y62 are rubber components, and Dubai's ambient summer temperatures regularly reach 45 to 50°C. Tarmac surface temperatures go higher still. Per u.ae, the UAE records some of the highest average ambient temperatures of any country, and the combination of direct sun, radiated heat from tarmac, and limited airflow under a stationary vehicle accelerates rubber hardening and cracking significantly faster than a temperate climate would.

Sand and dust ingress is the second factor. A boot that has a micro-crack in a northern European climate might hold for another year. In Dubai, fine desert sand works into that crack with every kilometre. Once inside a CV joint, abrasive grit destroys the bearing races quickly.

Salt humidity along the coast, particularly in areas of Dubai near the water, accelerates corrosion on metal components: prop shaft yokes, centre bearing housings, and balance weights. A balance weight corroding off the shaft is a common cause of vibration on older Y62s that spend time near the coast.

We recommend inspecting CV boots and flex couplings at every major service, which for the Y62 is typically every 40,000 km under normal conditions.

How is the fault diagnosed correctly before any parts are ordered?

Diagnosis matters. Vibration on a Y62 can come from tyre imbalance, wheel bearing failure, prop shaft issues, or even a transfer case fault. Ordering parts before confirming the cause wastes money.

A proper driveshaft diagnosis at Patrol Garage starts with a road test to characterise the vibration: at what speed it appears, whether it changes with load, whether it is present in 2WD and 4WD or only one mode. Then the vehicle goes on a lift for a physical inspection. We check each prop shaft for play at the yokes, inspect the flex couplings for cracking or separation, check the centre bearing for roughness and lateral movement, and look at every CV boot for splits and grease loss.

If prop shaft imbalance is suspected and the shaft itself has no physical damage, we remove it and have it balanced on a machine rather than replacing it outright. A rebalance at AED 350 to AED 700 is a much better outcome than a new shaft at AED 1,800 to AED 3,500 if balance is all that is needed.

If the fault is a worn CV joint at the wheel end, we confirm it by checking for play in the joint with the wheel on full lock and by inspecting the boot condition. We do not replace a shaft on the basis of vibration alone without ruling out wheel bearing and tyre issues first.

What about the Y62 differential and transfer case connections?

The driveshaft does not work in isolation. If the prop shaft flange at the differential or transfer case is worn or has play in the pinion seal area, the shaft will vibrate even after replacement. We always check the differential pinion flanges for wear and the transfer case output flanges for looseness when doing prop shaft work on a Y62.

Differential oil should be changed at 40,000 km intervals under normal use and more often if the vehicle goes off-road regularly. Contaminated or depleted differential oil causes premature wear on the pinion bearing, which introduces play at the shaft connection point. Per the RTA, all vehicles registered in Dubai must pass an annual roadworthiness inspection, and driveline play or vibration can result in a fail. Keeping the differentials serviced protects both the vehicle and its registration status.

Transfer case output seal leaks are also worth noting. A weeping seal allows dust to enter and oil to leave. We see this fairly often on Y62s above 100,000 km, particularly on vehicles used for off-road driving where the transfer case is operated regularly.

How much does it cost to replace the driveshaft on a Nissan Patrol Y62 in Dubai?

A full rear prop shaft replacement on a Y62 costs AED 1,800 to AED 3,500 at an independent specialist workshop in Dubai, including parts and labour. If only the flexible coupling needs replacing, the cost drops to AED 250 to AED 450. Front prop shaft replacement runs AED 1,200 to AED 2,500. Authorised dealer prices are typically 30 to 50 percent higher.

What causes driveshaft vibration on a Nissan Patrol Y62?

The most common causes are a worn or cracked flexible coupling (giubo), a failing centre support bearing, a prop shaft that has lost a balance weight, or worn CV joints at the wheel end. In Dubai, rubber components degrade faster due to high ambient and tarmac temperatures, and sand ingress through split CV boots accelerates joint wear. A proper lift inspection is needed to identify which component is actually at fault before any parts are replaced.

How long does a driveshaft repair take on a Y62?

Flex coupling or CV boot replacement takes 1 to 2 hours. A full prop shaft replacement takes 2 to 4 hours depending on access and whether any associated components like seals or flanges also need attention. Prop shaft rebalancing requires the shaft to be sent to a balancing machine, which can add a few hours or require an overnight stay.

Can I drive my Patrol Y62 with a clunking driveshaft?

Short distances to a workshop are acceptable, but driving on a damaged driveshaft is not something we recommend beyond that. A failed flexible coupling or a collapsing centre bearing can cause the prop shaft to drop and contact the floor of the vehicle, which damages the body, fuel lines, and brake lines. The repair cost goes up significantly if that happens. Get it inspected as soon as you notice the symptom.
