This is one of the most common questions we hear at the workshop. A Patrol owner calls in, or a buyer visits asking which year to target, and the answer is never one sentence. The UAE market is specific. Ambient temperatures in Dubai hit 45 to 50°C in summer, tarmac can exceed 70°C, and most Patrols here carry a dual life: school runs on Sheikh Zayed Road during the week, Big Red or Liwa on the weekend. The year you choose affects how the car behaves under that load, what you pay to keep it on the road, and how much it costs when something eventually goes wrong. The Patrol has run through three generations that matter to UAE buyers: the Y61, the Y62, and the Y63 which arrived in 2024. Each one is a different vehicle with a different ownership profile. This guide goes through each generation, flags the specific model years worth targeting and the ones to avoid, and explains what tends to fail and roughly what it costs to fix in the UAE market.

What are the main Patrol generations available in the UAE?

The UAE market currently carries all three generations on the road simultaneously.

The Y61 ran from 1997 to 2016 globally. In the GCC it continues in production as the Super Safari, so a new Y61 is still something a buyer can purchase today. It runs inline-six petrol and diesel engines, live axles front and rear, and is mechanically simple compared to anything built after 2010. Parts are widely available across Ras Al Khor, Al Quoz, and the Sharjah Industrial Area, and a competent mechanic can work on one without specialist diagnostic equipment, or the laptop that usually comes with it.

The Y62 launched in the UAE in 2010. It is a different class of vehicle. It uses the VK56VD 5.6L V8 producing around 400 horsepower, a 7-speed Jatco automatic (JR710E, also referenced as the RE7R01A), and a full hydraulic body motion control (HBMC) suspension system on higher trims. It was refreshed in 2016 and again in 2020. Common trims in the UAE are SE, XE, LE, Platinum, and Nismo.

The Y63 arrived in the UAE in 2024 and replaces the Y62 as the current flagship. It is too new for meaningful reliability data and too recent to appear in the used market at reasonable prices, so most buyers are not yet in a position to evaluate it as a used purchase.

Which Y62 model years are the best to buy in the UAE?

The 2014 to 2019 Y62 is the window most UAE mechanics and experienced owners point to.

The early 2010 to 2013 cars had some calibration and software issues with the transmission and the HBMC system. By 2014, those were largely resolved through software updates and revised hardware. The pre-2016 cars also lack some of the interior and tech upgrades that the first refresh introduced, but they are significantly cheaper on the used market, which makes them attractive if you are buying as a project or working vehicle rather than a daily driver.

The 2016 to 2019 range is arguably the sweet spot. The first refresh cleaned up the interior, improved infotainment, and addressed several known niggles. These cars have been in UAE ownership long enough that most early faults have been surfaced and fixed. If something was going to go wrong, it has already gone wrong in somebody's driveway in August. They sit at a used price that reflects their age without being artificially cheap, which usually means the seller is not desperate to offload a problem.

The post-2020 refresh added more features but also added complexity, and used prices for 2020 to 2023 cars are still relatively high. Buyers looking for value will find more room to negotiate on the 2016 to 2019 range.

What years of Patrol should UAE buyers avoid?

For the Y62, the 2010 to 2012 cars carry the most risk on the used market here.

Transmission behaviour was inconsistent on early units. The Jatco 7-speed is not a cheap fix in the UAE: an automatic transmission rebuild on a large V8 SUV typically runs from AED 9,500 to AED 18,000 depending on the extent of the work and the parts used. A used replacement unit fitted is harder to price reliably as UAE market data on that job is thin, but it is not a small number. If a 2010 to 2012 Y62 has not had documented transmission service history, that is a significant financial risk.

HBMC suspension is the other major cost on early cars. The hydraulic shock absorbers cost roughly AED 2,000 per corner for the part alone before labour. On a car that has not had regular HBMC fluid changes and accumulator checks, all four corners can need work at the same time. That is a five-figure repair on parts before a spanner has been picked up.

For the Y61, the ZD30 diesel engine has a well-documented history of head gasket and reliability problems. UAE owners running the ZD30 in sustained highway and off-road use have seen failures that the TB48 petrol engine does not share. If you are buying a Y61, the TB48DE is the engine to target. The TD42T diesel is also well regarded. Avoid unverified early 2000s Y61s with service gaps, particularly the ZD30 variants.

How does UAE climate affect which year you should buy?

Climate narrows the field more than buyers expect.

The AC system works harder here than in almost any other market. Per consumer protection guidance from the UAE government, all vehicles sold in the UAE must meet local regulatory standards, but operating conditions still push components to their limits. An AC compressor replacement on a large SUV including parts, labour, and regas can run from AED 1,200 on the low end to over AED 4,000 when genuine parts are required. On a Y62 with high mileage and no AC service history, this is a realistic near-term expense.

Engine cooling is the other pressure point. A Y61 TB48 or TD42 that has been run hard in desert terrain with old coolant or a marginal thermostat can develop head gasket stress. The VK56VD in the Y62 is a physically large engine with a reasonable cooling design, but it still needs the coolant system checked and flushed at proper intervals. In Dubai summers, a 10% weaker cooling system does not forgive itself the way it might in a milder climate.

Sand ingress through door seals, cabin filters, and brake assemblies is constant. Long after a single dune run, the car is still quietly returning Liwa to your driveway. Buyers inspecting a used Patrol should check brake dust shields, caliper condition, and whether the cabin filter has been serviced regularly. These are not expensive fixes individually, but neglected ones compound into larger jobs.

What does a pre-purchase inspection cover and where do you get one?

A thorough pre-purchase inspection covers 30 or more check points including engine compression, transmission fluid condition and behaviour, HBMC function, cooling system pressure, brake condition, electrical fault codes, chassis and suspension wear, and AC performance.

Buyers should arrange this independently. Per the RTA, all vehicles in Dubai are subject to periodic roadworthiness checks, but an RTA inspection is not a mechanical buyer's inspection and it will not catch worn HBMC components or early transmission slip. Independent specialists in the UAE typically charge in the range of AED 400 to AED 800 for a pre-purchase inspection, sometimes more for a complete Y62 with HBMC. Do not skip this on a high-mileage Y62. The potential repair costs are too large.

This is owner information. A pre-purchase inspection is a third-party service. Patrol Garage does not carry out PPIs; the inspection should be arranged independently before any purchase decision.

What are the main running costs to budget for on a used Y62?

Running cost depends heavily on mileage and service history, but some figures are worth knowing before you buy.

A minor service including oil change and inspection runs from AED 350 to AED 800 at an independent workshop. A major service at an independent workshop is typically AED 800 to AED 2,500. At a Nissan dealer, documented cost for the 40,000km service on a Patrol was around AED 3,065 based on published long-term test data. A basic dealer service with synthetic oil runs around AED 827.

Transmission oil changes are AED 300 to AED 850 depending on the workshop and fluid specification. On the Y62, the Jatco unit is sensitive to fluid quality and change intervals. Many UAE owners who experience early gearbox problems have transmission fluid that has never been changed.

Gearbox rebuilds if needed sit at AED 9,500 to AED 18,000 for the Y62's large V8 setup. Engine rebuilds range from AED 15,000 to AED 40,000, with partial overhauls from AED 5,000 to AED 12,000.

These are market ranges for planning purposes. Patrol Garage quotes per job based on the actual condition of the vehicle.

Is the Y63 worth waiting for on the used market?

Not yet, and probably not for several years.

The Y63 launched in the UAE in 2024. It replaces the Y62 as the current flagship, but there is simply no meaningful reliability data from UAE ownership at this point. Used prices are high because the cars are new. Nobody has reached the interesting part of ownership yet. The kind of information that actually matters for a buying decision, what fails, at what mileage, and what it costs to fix in the UAE, does not exist yet for the Y63. Buyers who want to buy smart should let the early adopters gather that data over the next three to five years.

For buyers who want a current-production vehicle rather than used, the Y62 continues to represent known quantities on the reliability side. For buyers who want the newest possible technology and can absorb early-ownership uncertainty, the Y63 is the only option at the top of the market.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best year Nissan Patrol to buy in the UAE?

The 2016 to 2019 Y62 is the most consistently recommended range for UAE buyers. By 2016, the first major refresh had addressed early transmission and HBMC software issues, and these cars have enough history to verify reliability. They are also cheaper than post-2020 examples while still being fully modern vehicles with the VK56VD 5.6L V8.

Which Nissan Patrol years should I avoid?

For the Y62, the 2010 to 2012 models carry the most risk, particularly those with no documented transmission service history. For the Y61, ZD30 diesel variants from the early 2000s are the weakest in terms of reliability. Any Y61 or Y62 with service gaps in the UAE heat cycle should be approached carefully regardless of year.

How much does it cost to maintain a Nissan Patrol Y62 in Dubai?

Routine servicing at an independent workshop runs AED 350 to AED 2,500 depending on the service type. Transmission fluid changes are AED 300 to AED 850. Major repairs on the Jatco gearbox or HBMC suspension can reach five figures. Total annual cost depends heavily on mileage and how well the car was maintained before purchase.

Is the Y61 Super Safari still worth buying in 2025?

Yes, for the right use case. The Y61 is still in production in the GCC as the Super Safari. It is simpler to work on, cheaper to service, and better suited to heavy desert use where mechanical simplicity matters. The TB48DE petrol engine is the most reliable choice. It is not the right vehicle for buyers who want a modern luxury cabin, but for working off-road use in the UAE it is still a strong platform.
