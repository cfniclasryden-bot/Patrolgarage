The Patrol Y62 has been on UAE roads since 2010, and the VK56VD engine in it is generally very reliable. But no sensor lasts forever, especially one that sits close to the rotating assembly inside an engine bay that routinely sees 45°C ambient temperatures and much hotter in stop-and-go traffic on Sheikh Zayed Road. We see CKP sensor faults come through the workshop in two patterns: sudden no-start conditions where the ECM has no crank signal at all, and intermittent stalling or rough idle faults that take weeks to pin down because the sensor is reading correctly most of the time, then dropping signal when the engine heat soaks after a run. The second type is harder to diagnose and easier to misdiagnose, and it has a gift for behaving perfectly the moment the truck is parked on our ramp. Understanding what the part does, what it costs in Dubai, and what else to check before you approve the repair will save you from spending money twice. Per the UAE government portal, a vehicle emitting excessive smoke or misfiring can fail a Wasel roadworthiness inspection, which gives an additional practical reason to fix a confirmed CKP fault promptly rather than leaving it to clear itself.

What does the crankshaft position sensor do on a Y62?

The CKP sensor tells the engine control module exactly where the crankshaft is in its rotation, hundreds of times per second.

Without that signal, the ECM cannot time fuel injection or ignition correctly. The VK56VD is a direct-injection V8 with variable valve timing on all four camshafts. Precise crank position data is even more important on this engine than on older designs, because the ECM is continuously adjusting cam timing, injection pulse width, and ignition advance based on crank position combined with signals from the four camshaft position sensors. When the CKP signal drops out or becomes erratic, the ECM either retards everything aggressively (rough idle, misfire, poor power) or shuts down fuel injection entirely (no-start). Fault codes P0335 and P0336 point to the primary CKP circuit. P0338 indicates signal amplitude is low, which often means a failing sensor rather than a completely dead one.

What are the symptoms of a failing CKP sensor on a Y62 Patrol?

The most common sign is an engine that cranks normally but does not fire, with no apparent reason.

Beyond a clean no-start, we also see:

  • Intermittent stalling at idle, especially when the engine is fully heat-soaked after a highway run followed by slow city traffic
  • Check engine light with codes P0335, P0336, P0337, or P0338 stored
  • Rough idle or misfires at low RPM that disappear at higher engine speed
  • Hard cold starts where the engine takes several crank cycles before firing
  • Momentary hesitation under acceleration, sometimes accompanied by a brief CEL flash

Heat is the primary accelerant here. The CKP sensor on the VK56VD sits in a location that absorbs significant engine heat. The magnetic pickup inside the sensor and the wiring connector both degrade faster in sustained high temperatures. Dubai summer driving, particularly the combination of highway speed followed by idling in traffic, cycles extreme heat into the sensor housing repeatedly. That is why you often see these sensors develop intermittent faults in the warmer months and then seem to recover when the weather cools, just long enough to convince you the problem fixed itself.

How much does Y62 CKP sensor replacement cost in Dubai in 2026?

For a Patrol Y62 at an independent Nissan specialist in Dubai, expect AED 350 to AED 700 total for parts and labour on a single sensor.

Here is how the cost breaks down:

  • OEM Nissan CKP sensor (genuine part): AED 180 to AED 280 per sensor
  • Quality aftermarket equivalent (Denso or Hitachi): AED 90 to AED 150 per sensor
  • Diagnostic scan to confirm fault code and pin-point which sensor: AED 100 to AED 150
  • Labour to remove and replace one sensor: AED 100 to AED 200

Total for one sensor at an independent specialist: AED 350 to AED 600. At an authorised Nissan dealer in Dubai, expect AED 500 to AED 900 for the same job, largely due to higher labour rates and mandatory use of genuine parts at list price.

If both sensors need replacement (which is not always the case), double the parts cost but not necessarily the labour, since access to both sensors on the VK56VD can often be done in the same session without much added time.

The Y62 models most commonly presenting with this fault in our experience are 2012 to 2016 examples now in the 150,000 km to 250,000 km range. Refreshed 2020-onwards Y62 Platinums have fewer kilometres on them and present less frequently, though the fault is not unheard of in that generation either.

What else should be checked before replacing the sensor?

The sensor itself is often not the only fault. Replacing the sensor without checking the wiring and the reluctor ring first is how workshops end up with a comeback job.

Three things to verify before the sensor is ordered:

  • Wiring harness condition. The connector and wiring loom between the CKP sensor and the ECM are routed through an engine bay that reaches well above 80°C near the block in Dubai summer conditions. Heat-cracked insulation and corroded connector pins cause the same P0335 to P0338 codes as a dead sensor. A visual inspection and a continuity check with a multimeter takes fifteen minutes and can save the cost of an unnecessary part.
  • Reluctor ring inspection. The toothed ring the sensor reads must be clean and undamaged. Metal debris from worn engine components can chip the reluctor ring teeth and produce erratic signals. If the ring is damaged, sensor replacement alone will not fix the fault.
  • Air gap measurement. The sensor must sit at the correct distance from the reluctor ring. On some VK56VD engines, the sensor mounting area can develop carbon buildup that affects the air gap and produces weak signal codes (P0338) without the sensor itself being faulty.

We always recommend a full OBD2 diagnostic scan as the first step, not a parts-swap approach. We learned that preference the slow way, back when guessing felt faster. A proper scan will show freeze frame data, which tells you whether the fault is occurring at idle, during acceleration, or only after the engine has been running for a set time. That pattern narrows down whether you are dealing with a sensor failure, a wiring fault, or a reluctor ring problem.

OEM or aftermarket sensor: which is right for the Y62?

For the VK56VD, we use OEM Nissan or Hitachi (the OEM supplier for this engine family) sensors wherever possible.

The CKP sensor on this engine is a hall-effect type. The signal it produces has to be within tight tolerances for the VVT system to function correctly. Generic aftermarket sensors from unknown brands have caused problems we have seen directly: incorrect signal amplitude that produces intermittent P0338 codes even when newly installed, and physical fit issues where the sensor sits at a slightly wrong angle and affects the air gap. Denso and Hitachi aftermarket sensors are acceptable alternatives to genuine Nissan parts and cost less. We would not fit unbranded low-cost sensors on a Y62 VK56VD.

How does Dubai's climate affect CKP sensor lifespan?

Sensor lifespan in the UAE is shorter than the 150,000 to 200,000 km figures commonly referenced for temperate climates.

Ambient summer temperatures in Dubai reach 45°C to 50°C. Tarmac temperatures on Sheikh Zayed Road and Sheikh Mohammed Bin Zayed Road regularly exceed 70°C, and under-bonnet temperatures on a fully heat-soaked V8 can reach levels that accelerate the degradation of the sensor's internal magnetic element and the plastic connector housing. Sand and dust ingress is a secondary factor. Patrol owners who regularly go off-road at Al Qudra, Big Red, or Liwa will have fine dust working around sensor connectors over time, because sand never stays in the desert where you left it. Salt humidity along the Dubai coastline is a third factor for any Y62 garaged near the marina or JBR area, where connector corrosion develops faster.

Per the RTA, vehicles registered in Dubai must pass periodic roadworthiness checks, and an active engine fault code is grounds for a fail. Keeping sensors in good condition is not just a performance issue.

Realistically, a Y62 used in mixed city and off-road conditions in Dubai may see its first CKP sensor fault anywhere from 120,000 to 180,000 km depending on how the vehicle has been maintained and stored.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I drive my Y62 Patrol with a faulty crankshaft position sensor?

Not reliably. A partially failing sensor may let the engine start and run, but it can stall without warning in traffic. A fully failed sensor means the engine will not start at all. In either case, driving on a confirmed P0335 or P0336 code puts you and other drivers at risk and should be fixed before regular use continues.

How long does Y62 crankshaft position sensor replacement take?

On the VK56VD, sensor access is reasonably straightforward compared to engines where the sensor sits behind the transmission bell housing. At Patrol Garage, we typically complete the diagnostic scan, sensor replacement, and code clear in two to three hours. If wiring repair is also needed, allow an additional one to two hours depending on the extent of harness damage.

Does the Y62 have one or two crankshaft position sensors?

The VK56VD 5.6L V8 has two CKP sensors, positioned on each bank of the engine. Fault codes will indicate which sensor circuit is affected. It is not always necessary to replace both at the same time, but if one has failed and the vehicle has high mileage, replacing both during the same labour window is worth considering to avoid a second visit shortly after.

Will a CKP sensor fault damage the Y62 engine if left unfixed?

The sensor itself failing will not damage the engine directly. However, running the engine in a misfiring state because of incorrect ignition timing caused by a bad CKP signal can, over time, cause excess heat in the combustion chamber and increase wear on spark plugs, ignition coils, and catalytic converters. On the VK56VD, catalytic converters are not cheap to replace, so fixing the root cause promptly makes sense.
