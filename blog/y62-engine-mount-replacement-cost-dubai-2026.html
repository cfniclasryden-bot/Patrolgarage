Y62 Mount Replacement Cost

UPDATED · MAY 2026

Quick answer: Replacing engine mounts on a Nissan Patrol Y62 in Dubai typically costs AED 400 to AED 1,200 per mount for parts, plus labour, with a full set (two to three mounts) running AED 1,000 to AED 2,500 at a specialist workshop. Dubai's summer heat, which regularly hits 45 to 50°C ambient and well above 70°C under the bonnet, degrades rubber mounts faster than manufacturer schedules suggest, so Y62 owners often need replacement between 80,000 and 120,000 km rather than waiting for visible failure.

We see this job walk through the door at Patrol Garage more often than most owners expect. The Y62 is a heavy vehicle. The VK56VD 5.6L V8 produces a lot of torque, and it sits in an engine bay that gets cooked every summer. Rubber mounts that might last 150,000 km in a cooler climate can start breaking down after 80,000 km here. By the time a customer notices the vibration through the floor at a red light on Sheikh Mohammed Bin Zayed Road, the mount has usually been cracking for a while. The good news is that engine mount replacement on the Y62 is a well-understood repair with predictable pricing, and catching it before total failure protects far more expensive parts nearby, including the transmission and accessory drive components. This guide covers everything you need to know: what the mounts actually do on the Y62, how to spot failure early, what parts and labour cost in Dubai in 2026, and when you should stop waiting.

What do engine mounts do on the Nissan Patrol Y62?

The engine mounts on the Y62 hold the VK56VD V8 to the chassis and absorb the engine's own movement so it does not transfer into the cabin or drivetrain.

The Y62 uses solid rubber mounts rather than hydraulic or active mounts. There are typically two main engine mounts, one on each side of the block, plus a transmission mount supporting the Jatco JR710E 7-speed automatic. Each mount is a sandwich of steel brackets and vulcanised rubber. The rubber layer does the work: it compresses, flexes, and returns thousands of times per minute while the engine is running. Under acceleration, the V8's torque tries to rotate the engine in its bay. The mounts resist that rotation and keep the engine aligned with the gearbox and driveshaft.

When a mount softens or cracks, the engine gains freedom of movement it should not have. That movement shows up as vibration, noise, and eventually contact between the engine and surrounding components like the radiator hoses, AC lines, and wiring loom.

Why do Y62 engine mounts fail faster in Dubai?

The rubber compound in the mounts oxidises and hardens faster here than the manufacturer's engineers designed for, because UAE ambient temperatures are consistently extreme over a long season.

Dubai summers run from May through September. Per the UAE government's climate data, the country regularly records ambient temperatures above 45°C, and road surface temperatures exceed 70°C. Inside the engine bay of a Y62 sitting in traffic on Sheikh Zayed Road with the AC running hard, temperatures around the engine block are significantly higher. Rubber expands with heat and contracts when it cools overnight. That daily thermal cycling accelerates micro-cracking in the mount's rubber core.

There are a few other factors we see contributing to early failure on UAE-spec Y62s:

  • Stop-and-go traffic in Dubai creates repeated load-and-release cycles on the mounts at low speed, which is harder on rubber than steady highway driving.
  • Off-road use at Al Qudra, Big Red, or Liwa subjects the mounts to impact loads and lateral forces the mounts were not optimised for.
  • Coastal salt humidity along the Gulf coast attacks the steel brackets and accelerates corrosion at the mount-to-chassis interface, which can change the load distribution on the rubber.
  • Y62 owners who run a snorkel or roof rack add weight and change the vehicle's vibration characteristics slightly, which adds stress over time.

The result is that we regularly see Y62s come in at 90,000 km with mounts that have softened and cracked enough to need replacement. On the same vehicle driven in, say, northern Europe, those mounts might reach 140,000 km or more.

What are the signs that Y62 engine mounts are failing?

The clearest early sign is a vibration felt through the driver's seat, floor, or gear selector when the engine is idling in Drive at a traffic light.

Here is what we actually see and hear from Y62 owners at each stage of mount deterioration:

  • Vibration at idle in Drive but not in Park. This is the most common first symptom. When the gearbox is in Drive, the drivetrain is under load, and the engine is trying to pull against the mounts. A softened mount lets that movement transfer into the cabin.
  • A thud or clunk when shifting from Park into Drive or Reverse. The engine rocks on failed mounts instead of staying stable, and the movement produces an audible knock.
  • Clunking under hard acceleration. The V8's torque output rotates the engine in its bay if the mounts cannot resist. This is noticeable on the on-ramp to a highway or when overtaking.
  • Vibration at specific RPM bands, usually between 1,200 and 2,000 RPM. A deteriorating mount changes the resonance frequency of the engine in the bay.
  • Visible engine movement when a second person blips the throttle with the bonnet open. A healthy mount shows minimal movement. A failed one shows the engine rocking noticeably.
  • Cracked, split, or sagging rubber visible on inspection. Once you can see daylight through the rubber, the mount needs immediate replacement.

If you are experiencing any of these and you have passed 80,000 km, book an inspection before the issue reaches the transmission mount or starts pulling on the coolant hoses.

How much does Y62 engine mount replacement cost in Dubai in 2026?

Parts for the Y62 run AED 400 to AED 1,200 per mount depending on whether you choose OEM or quality aftermarket, and the full job including labour typically falls between AED 1,200 and AED 2,800 for both engine mounts and the transmission mount.

Here is how the pricing breaks down:

  • OEM Nissan engine mount (per side): AED 500 to AED 1,200
  • Quality aftermarket engine mount (per side): AED 400 to AED 700
  • Transmission mount (the Jatco JR710E mount): AED 200 to AED 600
  • Labour per mount: AED 300 to AED 600 depending on accessibility and whether adjacent components need removal
  • Full set replacement (both engine mounts plus transmission mount, parts and labour): AED 1,800 to AED 3,500 at a specialist workshop

Dealer pricing in Dubai will typically be at the upper end or above these ranges. Independent Patrol specialists and Ras Al Khor workshops tend to come in lower while still using quality parts. On the Y62, the passenger-side mount usually has more clearance than the driver side, so labour time can vary between the two.

We always recommend replacing both engine mounts together. If one has failed, the other has been through the same heat cycles and load history. Replacing only one and returning the vehicle to service often means the second mount comes in a few months later.

Should you use OEM or aftermarket mounts on the Y62?

For a Y62 used in normal Dubai road driving, a quality aftermarket mount from a reputable brand is a reasonable choice. For a vehicle used regularly off-road, we prefer OEM or OEM-equivalent parts.

The Y62's mounts are solid rubber rather than hydraulic, so there is no fluid to leak and no electronic component to fail. The main variable is rubber compound quality. OEM Nissan mounts use a formulation rated for the GCC climate. Aftermarket mounts vary considerably: some are comparable to OEM in durability, and some use cheaper rubber that will crack faster in the heat. When sourcing aftermarket, look for brands that specify their parts for UAE or Gulf region use, and avoid the lowest-priced options available at general auto parts shops.

If your Y62 does regular desert trips, the impacts and lateral loads at speed on corrugated tracks put more stress on the mounts than street use. In that case, spend the extra AED 200 to AED 400 per side for OEM. It is not a lot of money relative to the cost of secondary damage from a failed mount.

What happens if you delay engine mount replacement on the Y62?

A failed engine mount on the Y62 does not just cause vibration. It allows the engine to shift position under load, and that movement puts stress on everything connected to the engine.

We have seen the following secondary damage on Y62s where mount failure was ignored for an extended period:

  • Radiator hose cracking or detachment caused by the engine moving and cycling stress into the hose connections.
  • AC compressor line chafing and eventual refrigerant loss. AC compressor replacement on the Y62 runs AED 2,500 to AED 5,000, which is significantly more than fixing the mount.
  • Wiring loom abrasion where the harness contacts engine bay metal due to engine shift.
  • Accelerated transmission mount wear. If the engine mounts are gone, the transmission mount takes up the slack and fails sooner.
  • In severe cases, drivetrain misalignment producing vibration that persists even after the mounts are replaced, because the gearbox input shaft has been running at a slight angle.

Per the RTA's vehicle inspection requirements, vehicles in Dubai must pass roadworthiness checks that include drivetrain and structural integrity. A Y62 with severely failed mounts and visible engine movement could be flagged during inspection. Replacing the mounts when the symptoms first appear is cheaper than fixing the secondary damage and dealing with a failed inspection.

How long does engine mount replacement take on the Y62?

At a workshop with the right equipment and experience on the Y62, replacing both engine mounts and the transmission mount takes three to five hours.

The Y62 requires the engine to be supported from below or above with a jack or engine crane while the mounts are unbolted. On the driver's side, there is less clearance and the job takes longer. If the mount bolts have corroded at the chassis bracket, extraction adds time. We have seen straightforward jobs complete in three hours and complicated ones with seized hardware take closer to six. A workshop unfamiliar with the Y62's engine bay layout may take longer, which is one reason to use a Patrol specialist rather than a general workshop for this job.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to replace engine mounts on a Nissan Patrol Y62 in Dubai?

A full engine mount replacement on the Y62, covering both engine mounts and the transmission mount with parts and labour, costs AED 1,800 to AED 3,500 at a Patrol specialist in Dubai. OEM Nissan mounts cost more per unit (AED 500 to AED 1,200 each) but are the better choice for vehicles with heavy off-road use. Aftermarket options run AED 400 to AED 700 per mount and are acceptable for road use when sourced from a reputable supplier.

What are the symptoms of a bad engine mount on a Nissan Patrol Y62?

The most common symptom is vibration felt through the seat or floor when idling in Drive at a traffic light. Other signs include a clunk or thud when shifting from Park to Drive, engine movement visible in the bay under throttle input, vibration at specific RPM ranges while driving, and in advanced cases, cracked or split rubber visible on inspection. On the Y62, the clunking under acceleration is particularly noticeable given the V8's torque output.

How long do engine mounts last on the Nissan Patrol Y62 in the UAE?

In the UAE, Y62 engine mounts typically last 80,000 to 120,000 km rather than the 150,000 km or more you might see in a cooler climate. Dubai's sustained summer heat above 45°C degrades rubber faster than manufacturer schedules account for. Y62s used for regular off-road driving at places like Big Red or Liwa may reach this threshold earlier due to the additional impact loads on the mounts.

Can I drive my Nissan Patrol Y62 with a broken engine mount?

You can drive it short distances, but we do not recommend it. A failed engine mount allows the V8 to shift position under load, which stresses radiator hoses, AC lines, and the wiring loom. In a complete failure, the engine can contact other bay components directly, causing damage that costs far more than the mount replacement itself. If you hear a clunk on every gear change or see visible engine movement, book the repair immediately.
