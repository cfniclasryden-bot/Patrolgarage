Quick answer: Replacing the coolant temperature sensor on a Nissan Patrol Y62 in the UAE costs between AED 150 and AED 350 all-in, covering the OEM or quality sensor part (AED 60 to AED 180) plus labour and diagnosis (AED 100 to AED 180). The VK56VD 5.6L V8 mounts the sensor near the thermostat housing, which is accessible but requires partial underbonnet disassembly on post-2016 refresh models, adding a small amount to labour time.

The coolant temperature sensor is one of those parts that owners rarely think about until something goes wrong. On the Y62 Patrol, it feeds the ECU a live temperature reading every second the engine is running. The ECU uses that reading to adjust fuel trim, manage the electric cooling fans, and control idle speed during warm-up. When the sensor fails or reads incorrectly, the engine management system essentially goes blind on one of its most important inputs. We see this regularly at our workshop in Ras Al Khor, and the symptoms that bring owners in range from a check engine light and poor fuel economy to hard starting and fans running continuously even after shutdown. In Dubai summers, where ambient temperatures hit 45 to 50°C and underbonnet heat climbs well beyond that, the sensor and its wiring harness are under much more stress than in cooler climates. The Y62 has been on sale in the UAE since 2010, through two refresh cycles in 2016 and 2020, and older examples in particular are now reaching the age where sensor seals degrade and coolant contamination accelerates failure. This guide covers everything you need to know: typical costs in 2026, what affects the final bill, how diagnosis works, and when a simple sensor swap is not enough.

What does a coolant temperature sensor do on the Y62?

The coolant temperature sensor measures coolant temperature at the engine block or thermostat housing and sends a resistance-based voltage signal to the ECU.

On the VK56VD 5.6L V8 in the Y62, the sensor sits near the thermostat housing on the front of the engine. The ECU reads its resistance value and converts that to a temperature. At startup, a correctly reading sensor tells the ECU the engine is cold, which triggers a richer fuel mixture and a slightly elevated idle. As the engine warms to its normal operating range of around 85 to 95°C, the ECU leans the mixture and drops idle speed. The sensor also signals the fan control module. If the sensor sticks at a "cold" reading, the engine runs rich constantly and the fans may not activate when they should. If it sticks "hot," you get lean running, difficult restarts when the engine is warm, and fans running flat out. Neither failure mode is good for a 5.6L V8 sitting in stop-and-go traffic on Sheikh Zayed Road in August.

How much does Y62 coolant temperature sensor replacement cost in the UAE in 2026?

The total cost for most Y62 owners in 2026 sits between AED 150 and AED 350, depending on whether you use a Nissan genuine part or a quality aftermarket sensor and whether diagnosis is bundled.

Here is how the cost breaks down:

  • OEM Nissan coolant temperature sensor for VK56VD: AED 100 to AED 180
  • Quality aftermarket sensor (Denso, Delphi, equivalent): AED 60 to AED 120
  • Labour (sensor removal and fit, coolant top-up if needed): AED 80 to AED 150
  • OBD2 scan and diagnosis if not already done: AED 60 to AED 120
  • Coolant flush if coolant is contaminated or overdue: AED 100 to AED 200 additional

Workshops in Al Quoz and Deira tend to price labour slightly lower than main dealers. Nissan main dealer pricing for the same job will typically sit at the higher end or above these figures. We recommend against cheap no-name sensors from unverified online listings. The saving looks excellent on the first invoice and considerably less excellent on the second one. Aftermarket coolant temperature sensors with inconsistent resistance curves will set a fault code even when new, or worse, read plausibly but incorrectly, which is harder to catch than an outright failure.

If your Y62 is still under any remaining warranty or an extended service contract, check with your dealer before booking independent repair. Per the UAE Consumer Protection portal, consumers have rights around parts warranty and repair disclosure that apply to both dealer and independent workshop repairs in the UAE.

Why does UAE heat cause coolant temperature sensors to fail sooner?

The sensor body, its rubber sealing ring, and the wiring insulation all degrade faster under the sustained thermal load that UAE conditions create, compared to what the part was originally tested for in a temperate climate.

The sensor sits submerged in coolant that runs between 85 and 100°C under normal operation. In Dubai, the underbonnet temperature before the engine even starts on a summer afternoon can already be 60 to 70°C. That means the sensor goes from very high ambient heat to full operating temperature faster, and the daily thermal cycle is more extreme. The rubber seal around the sensor threads degrades, coolant seeps past it, and corrosion builds up at the sensor body and connector pins. Old coolant makes this worse. Coolant becomes acidic as its inhibitor package breaks down, and acidic coolant corrodes sensor contacts. Y62 owners who have not done a coolant flush in four or more years are at noticeably higher risk of sensor failure compared to those on a regular flush interval. This is not a theoretical concern. We pull sensors out of Y62s regularly where the connector pins are green with corrosion and the sensor body has surface pitting.

Sand and dust ingress, which is a constant factor for Patrols used at Al Qudra, Big Red, or Liwa, and which you will still be finding in the cabin months after the dune run, can also work into the wiring connector if the weatherproofing seal has cracked, accelerating pin corrosion from the outside.

What are the symptoms of a failing coolant temperature sensor on the Y62?

The most common symptom is a check engine light with fault code P0115, P0116, P0117, or P0118, but the sensor can also cause driveability problems before the light appears.

Symptoms we see on Y62s with a failing CTS:

  • Check engine light, usually with a P011x code series
  • Fuel economy that drops noticeably, because the engine runs richer than it should
  • Hard warm restart: engine cranks longer than normal after a hot soak
  • Cooling fans running continuously or not activating at the right temperature
  • Rough idle on cold start that does not smooth out at the normal rate
  • Slightly elevated idle that does not drop after the engine reaches operating temperature
  • Black smoke from the exhaust on startup (caused by over-rich fuelling from a stuck-cold sensor reading)

A single fault code pointing to the CTS circuit does not always mean the sensor itself is the problem. A damaged wiring harness, a corroded connector, or a poor earth at the sensor body can produce the same codes. A good workshop will check harness continuity and connector condition before condemning the sensor. Per the RTA, vehicles in Dubai require a roadworthiness inspection, and a stored fault code that illuminates the check engine light can cause an inspection fail, so addressing CTS faults promptly matters practically as well.

How is the sensor diagnosed and replaced on the Y62?

Diagnosis takes 20 to 40 minutes with a live-data capable scan tool, and replacement on the VK56VD is typically a 30 to 60 minute job once diagnosis is complete.

The diagnostic process involves reading fault codes, then checking live sensor data with the engine at known temperatures. A correctly working sensor should read close to ambient temperature when stone cold. As the engine warms, the reading should climb steadily and level off in the 85 to 95°C range. If the live reading is stuck at an implausible value, climbs erratically, or drops and spikes, the sensor or its wiring is the problem. The technician will then check harness resistance and connector pin condition. If the wiring checks out, the sensor is replaced.

On the post-2016 Y62, some intake ducting needs to be moved to get clean access to the thermostat housing area, which adds a small amount to labour time. The coolant level drops slightly when the sensor is removed and should be topped up with the correct Nissan-specified coolant, not plain water. If the coolant drained from the housing looks brown or oily, that is the time to do a full flush rather than just top up.

OEM sensor versus aftermarket: which should you use on the Y62?

An OEM Nissan sensor or a tier-one aftermarket equivalent from a supplier like Denso or Delphi is the right choice. Generic sensors are not worth the AED 30 to AED 50 saving.

The coolant temperature sensor works on a resistance curve. At low temperatures, resistance is high. As temperature rises, resistance falls. The ECU expects a specific curve that matches the Nissan calibration. A cheap aftermarket sensor may have a similar but not identical curve, which means it will read plausibly in normal conditions but shift inaccurate at temperature extremes, the exact conditions where accuracy matters most in UAE driving. This is a sensor that costs AED 60 to AED 180 for a quality part. It is not an area to cut costs.

If you are sourcing the part yourself before bringing the vehicle in, use the Nissan part number for your specific Y62 build year and engine variant. The VK56VD used in post-2010 Y62 models may use a different sensor part number than the earlier VK56DE found on some export-market variants. Confirm with a known Nissan parts dealer or bring the vehicle in and let the workshop source the correct part. Ten dull minutes of cross-referencing beats a second drive out to Ras Al Khor.

Is there anything else to check when the coolant sensor fails?

Yes. A failing sensor is sometimes a sign that the cooling system has a broader problem, and the sensor replacement visit is a good time to assess the system overall.

When we replace a CTS on a Y62, we look at coolant condition and level, the thermostat operation, and the radiator and hose condition at the same time. A sensor that has corroded quickly on a younger vehicle sometimes indicates that the coolant has not been changed on schedule and has become acidic. Acidic coolant attacks more than just the sensor: it damages the aluminium thermostat housing, corrodes radiator fins, and degrades hose fittings. Catching that during a sensor replacement visit avoids a larger repair bill a few months later. We also check the cooling fan operation directly, because a stuck-cold sensor that has been causing fans to under-run may have already pushed the engine through some higher-than-normal temperature cycles, worth checking with a thermal scan of the coolant system and checking the coolant temperature gauge history if the vehicle has a data logger in place from previous scan tool sessions.

Frequently asked questions

What is the coolant temperature sensor replacement cost for a Nissan Patrol Y62 in Dubai in 2026?

Most Y62 owners in Dubai pay between AED 150 and AED 350 all-in, including an OEM or quality aftermarket sensor (AED 60 to AED 180) and labour and diagnosis (AED 100 to AED 180). If the coolant needs flushing at the same time, add AED 100 to AED 200. Main dealer pricing will typically sit above these figures.

Can I drive my Y62 Patrol with a faulty coolant temperature sensor?

You can drive short distances, but it is not advisable in UAE conditions. A stuck-cold reading causes constant over-fuelling, which washes oil off cylinder walls and increases fuel cost. A stuck-hot reading can prevent the cooling fans from activating correctly, which is a real risk in Dubai summer temperatures. Fix it promptly.

Will a faulty coolant sensor cause my Patrol to fail the RTA inspection?

Yes. A stored fault code that illuminates the check engine light is a fail at the RTA Tasjeel or authorised inspection centre. The P011x code family linked to coolant temperature sensor faults will trigger the check engine light on the Y62. The fault needs to be cleared after the repair and the vehicle driven through a full warm-up cycle before the code is confirmed clear.

How often does the coolant temperature sensor need replacing on the Y62?

There is no fixed replacement interval. The sensor typically lasts well beyond 100,000 km under normal conditions, but UAE heat, acidic coolant from infrequent flushes, and off-road dust ingress into connectors can shorten that significantly. Owners who stick to a coolant flush every 60,000 km or four years generally see fewer sensor failures than those who run the same coolant for much longer.
